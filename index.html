CHRONICLES OF THE COSMOS // 4.6 BILLION YEARS

HISTORY OF THE SOLAR SYSTEM

4.6 BILLION YEARS AGO

The Solar Nebula Collapse

A nearby supernova shockwave triggered the gravitational collapse of a giant molecular cloud. Most mass gathered at the center, igniting nuclear fusion to birth our Sun, while the remaining gas and dust flattened into a spinning protoplanetary disk.

4.5 BILLION YEARS AGO

Accretion of Terrestrial & Jovian Worlds

Inside the frost line, rocky planetesimals collided to forge Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. Beyond the frost line, abundant ices allowed Jupiter and Saturn to rapidly capture massive hydrogen-helium envelopes, followed by ice giants Uranus and Neptune.

4.1 – 3.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

The Grand Tack & Late Heavy Bombardment

Gravitational migration of the giant planets reshaped the asteroid belt and hurled icy comets toward the inner worlds—delivering water to Earth's oceans and sculpting the cratered faces of Mercury and the Moon.

PRESENT & FUTURE

The Anthropic Era & Red Giant Horizon

Today, eight planets, dwarf worlds, and trillions of Kuiper Belt objects orbit in dynamic harmony. In roughly 5 billion years, the Sun will exhaust its core hydrogen and expand into a Red Giant, transforming the Solar System once more.
